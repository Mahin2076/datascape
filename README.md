# DataScape

Explore housing data by walking through a pixel city. Built for a one-day hackathon about creative ways to showcase data.

## The story

**Where is rent outpacing household income?** Compare San Francisco, New York, Austin, Chicago, and Seattle across the 2021–2024 Census ACS releases.

- Walk with WASD or arrow keys; Shift to run.
- Click streets to walk around buildings; press E near a door to inspect.
- Watch the story play through time, then reveal a sourced five-city comparison.
- Scrub the timeline to change growth bars and historical rent signs.
- Ask supported questions about rent growth and affordability.

Vanilla JavaScript, HTML, CSS, and Canvas. No API key, package install, or build step.

## Run locally

The compiled artwork atlases used by the app are included. From the repository root:

```sh
python3 -m http.server 8123 --directory dist
```

Open http://localhost:8123.

## City overview preview (`feature/map-zoom`)

San Francisco opens on an interactive Leaflet/OpenStreetMap street map with real roads and labels. Map tiles require an internet connection; no API key is needed. Click the map to open the expanded illustrative street world. Drag to pan or use +/− to zoom the overview. Click pavement to walk. Use the City overview button, M, Escape, or zoom out to return. Compare cities keeps the original five-city story available. Other cities currently use the schematic street scenes.

Resident clicks now emit stable selection IDs for the resident-profile branch; profiles and AI responses are not implemented here. See [map integration handoff](dist/map/README.md) for selection state, events, controls, asset provenance, and tests.

## Data and limits

`dist/assets/data.json` contains 20 source-linked city records. Each building represents its city's median gross rent, not an actual property listing. Streets and buildings are fictional. Income is median household income, not wages or youth income.

ACS releases are overlapping five-year estimates. Dollar changes are nominal, and statistical significance is not tested. Annual rent divided by median household income is a comparison proxy, not a measured renter burden. The app describes historical divergence, not its causes. Residents and traffic are decorative. Answers are calculated locally for supported questions, not generated by an AI model.
